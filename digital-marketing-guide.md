## Section 1 — Introduction

Marketing splits into systems. First system: **strategy**.

Strategy = market research done up front so you don't misfire the offer:

- Don't sell bird cream to kids.
- Don't show a photo of two happy couples to a divorced man.

Point: know the research cold *before* acting. Research tells you exactly what the next step is. Everything downstream (message, targeting, creative) rests on it.

## Section 2 — Market Research

Goal of this section: **find your audience.**

"Audience" here = the people/businesses who need the service I deliver. For my agency that service is building + managing websites for local businesses. So step one is identifying *which* businesses actually need a website built and managed.

### The audience-segment prompt

The course gave a prompt (course-only — link people to the course to get it). Fed it my agency/portfolio project so it could read what services I deliver, then it produced **three audience segments**:

**Segment 1 — Local service business owners** (Florida trades: HVAC, junk/car removal, cleaning, roofing)
Why: They run field work — no time or skill to touch a website. Hub model fits exactly: Ali hosts, they fire off AI change requests instead of learning a CMS. Local-SEO city pages (the `/locations/[city]` FL matrix) put them on the map for "near me" searches that drive their whole pipeline. Recurring plan = someone else owns uptime, edits, and ranking — the thing they can't do themselves.

**Segment 2 — Solo SaaS / early-stage founders** (pre-hire, one or two people)
Why: Need a marketing site + blog that ships changes weekly, but can't justify a full-time dev or eat a freelancer's per-change quotes. Hub gives them a managed CMS + AI change requests + built-in blog publishing — pricing tweaks, new landing sections, posts go live without a build step on their end. Monthly retainer beats hourly freelancers because their site changes constantly during launch phase.

**Segment 3 — Established small businesses stuck on an outdated, unmaintained site**
Why: Have a working business but a site nobody's touched in years — original builder vanished, in-house nobody knows the stack. Their need is offload, not learning. Hub's "set and forget" managed model (Ali handles hosting, domain via Vercel, edits via requests) removes the maintenance burden entirely. Predictable monthly cost + one point of contact is more attractive than a scary rebuild quote or hiring staff.

### Verifying the segments

Second prompt (also course-only): verify the three segments are real. AI returned multiple real websites/businesses matching each segment — confirmed the segments are genuine people/businesses operating this way. Worked well.

### Problem → solution → key message

Third prompt: for each segment, produce **problem, solution, and key message.**
Outcome: now I know who the audience is + how to write a marketing email/message per segment so they hire me. Strong section.

## Section 4 — SEO (Search Engine Optimization)

Four lessons: Keyword Research, SEO-Friendly Content, On-Page SEO, Backlinks. For each client, this is the checklist of what to do.

### Keyword Research
Find the terms clients' customers actually type into Google (e.g. "smart water bottle"). Look at who ranks now (competitors, Amazon) and the "People also ask" box for real questions to answer.
Course gave a keyword-research prompt — course-only, not shared here. See the course: https://www.udemy.com/course/digital-marketing-guide/
**Per client:** pull the keywords their buyers search, map them to pages (esp. the `/locations/[city]` matrix).

### SEO-Friendly Content
Write pages/posts around the target keyword so Google understands the page and it answers the searcher's intent.
**Per client:** each page targets one keyword; answer the "People also ask" questions in the copy.

### On-Page SEO
The on-page signals: title, meta description, headings (H1/H2), URL, image alt, internal links.
**Per client:** one keyword in the title (meta paraphrases it), clean URL, alt text, internal links between related pages.

### Backlinks
Other sites linking back = authority. Off-page. Slow but moves rankings.
**Per client:** get listed in local directories, get links from local partners/suppliers.

## Section 5 — Email Marketing

Three lessons: Email Setup, Capture Leads, Create Email Campaign. Tool of choice: **Resend** (marketing email) — cheaper than Mailchimp and other providers.

### Email Setup
Get a verified sending domain and connect the email tool.
**Per client:** set up Resend on the client's domain (SPF/DKIM/DMARC verified) so email lands in inbox, not spam.

### Capture Leads
Collect emails from site visitors — form, opt-in, lead magnet.
**Per client:** add a signup/lead form on the site, feed contacts into Resend.

### Create Email Campaign
Write and send the campaign to the list.
**Per client:** build the campaign in Resend, send to captured leads (use the per-segment message from Section 2).

## Section 10 — Influencer Marketing

(Course section 10 — jumped ahead; sections 7–9 pending.) Big section, 8 lessons. Highest ROI channel: ~$5.2 back per $1 vs ~$2.75 organic, ~$2.0 ads.

### Influencer Marketing Explained
An influencer gets people to buy by recommending on social. Influencer marketing = paying/partnering with them to sell your product.
**Per client:** for clients with a product/service that fits social, propose influencer promo as a high-ROI add-on.

### The Influencer Triangle
Win-win-win: **Business** has a product (needs an audience), **Influencer** has an audience (needs money/product), **Audience** has money (needs a reason to buy — a discount). Match all three.
**Per client:** only pitch influencers whose audience is the client's target customer.

### Find Influencers
Know the niche → find candidates that fit branding/product → check **engagement rate** (matters more than follower count: 5k @ 15% beats 50k @ 1.5%). Search Instagram by niche hashtag; vet profile for followers, niche fit, location, branding. IG dominates (~80% of influencers).
**Per client:** shortlist local/niche influencers, rank by engagement rate not follower count. Tool: Instagram engagement calculator.

### Paying Influencers
Levers to win them: money, free products, authority (boost credibility), meaning (shared values) — "why choose you?". Tiers: **Nano** 1k–10k ($10–100), **Micro** 10k–50k ($100–500), **Mid-tier** 50k–500k ($500–5k), **Macro** 500k–1M ($5k–10k), **Mega** 1M+ ($10k+). Cost also varies by content type (reel > carousel > photo > story). Affiliate route: choose platform → give affiliate links/discount codes → track sales (Upfluence, Afluencer).
**Per client:** start with nano/micro (cheap, high engagement); offer free product or affiliate code to keep cost down.

### Reach Out
Email (nano may need a DM). Personalized intro + show appreciation → suggest collab (short, gauge interest) → share business → focus on their work, not you.
**Per client:** send personalized outreach per influencer referencing a specific post; keep it short.

### Signing the Influencer
Create a paper trail (nano/micro don't need formal contracts) → negotiate terms → add requests (depends how you pay) → set a deadline that suits them → ask to be mentioned (tag, website link, business name).
**Per client:** confirm deliverables (e.g. 1 reel + 1 story + 1 post), deadline, and required tags/links in writing.

### Track & Measure
Use a CRM + a tracker (discount code, affiliate link, or UTM code). Minimum ROI of 2. Scalability over ROI — net profit is what matters ($5k spend @ ROI 2 = $10k beats $1k @ ROI 9 = $9k).
**Per client:** attach a UTM/discount code per influencer, track in GA4/CRM, report ROI.

### Create a Campaign
Full loop: set budget → find influencers (match type to budget) → reach out → sign → track & measure (aim ROI ≥ 2). Build tracked URLs with a UTM builder.
**Per client:** run the whole loop as a packaged campaign; report results back.

## Section 11 — Branding

(Ali knows branding — keep this section light. Big section, ~30 short lessons. Only "Branding vs Marketing" covered so far; rest come next.)

**Branding vs Marketing.** Branding = **what** you deliver: how people feel about you, who you are, what makes you unique, your look, values, message. Marketing = **how** you deliver: channels, communication, how you grow and make money (SEO, social, ads, website, print). Branding lasts; marketing changes. Branding comes first, then marketing. You need both — one without the other fails. Branding covers logo, values, audience, message, vision, mission, personality, positioning, trust, identity. Under branding: brand design, brand message, brand personality, brand positioning, internal communication.

The rest of the section walks these one at a time:
- **Foundation** — Why you need a brand strategy → structure of branding.
- **Purpose / Vision / Mission / Values** — each gets: why it matters → examples → write your own.
- **Positioning** — who your competitors are → what makes you different (differentiator) → your positioning statement.
- **Voice & personality** — brand personality → define it → find your brand voice.
- **Story** — power of brand storytelling → how to tell your brand story.
- **Identity assets** — name → tagline → colors → fonts → logo types → your logo.

**Per client:** run the brand basics before design — purpose, audience, values, positioning, voice — then lock the visual identity (name, colors, fonts, logo) so every page and campaign stays consistent.
