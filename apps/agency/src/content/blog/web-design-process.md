Every good website I've built followed the same **web design process** — four clear steps that take a project from a first conversation to a live site the client can run themselves. The process isn't there to slow things down. It's there so nothing important gets skipped, so you always know what happens next, and so the site that launches is the site we actually agreed on.

This post walks through all four steps: **Discovery, Strategy, Execution, and Launch & Scale.** Along the way I'll show you how I build — including how I use AI to work faster without cutting corners, and how the site I hand over is one you can edit yourself, no developer required.

## Step 1: Discovery

Every project starts with understanding, not design. Before I open a code editor or draw a single layout, I need to know what the website is actually for.

Discovery is a focused conversation about three things:

- **Your goals.** What should this website *do*? Book appointments, sell a service, collect leads, build credibility? A site built to book calls looks different from one built to sell products.
- **Your audience.** Who's visiting, and what do they need in the first ten seconds? A small-business owner comparing prices needs different things than someone who already trusts you and just wants to get started.
- **Your competition.** I look at what similar businesses are doing — what works, what's cluttered, and where there's room for you to stand out instead of blending in.

The output of Discovery isn't a design. It's clarity. By the end, we both know exactly what the site needs to accomplish and who it's speaking to. Everything after this step is easier because of it.

## Step 2: Strategy

With Discovery done, Strategy turns understanding into a plan. This is where the shape of the website appears — before any visuals, so we're agreeing on structure while it's still cheap to change.

Strategy covers the roadmap and the architecture:

- **Which pages exist, and why.** Most small-business sites need fewer pages than people expect. I'd rather build three pages that convert than ten that dilute the message.
- **The journey through the site.** What's the path from landing on the homepage to taking action? Every page should have one clear next step.
- **The creative direction.** The overall feel — typography, color, tone — is set here so the whole site is consistent instead of a patchwork of good-looking pieces that don't belong together.

Good strategy is what separates a website that just looks nice from one that actually earns its keep. It's the difference between decoration and design.

## Step 3: Execution

This is where the site gets built — and where I get the most questions, because how I build is a little different from a typical agency.

I write real, production-grade code on a modern stack — **Next.js, React, and Astro** — rather than assembling pages in a drag-and-drop builder. That matters because hand-controlled code is faster, more secure, and genuinely yours. Page builders trade long-term performance for short-term convenience; they bundle in bloat that slows the site down and can't be fully removed. A custom-built site loads fast, ranks well, and doesn't lock you into anyone's platform.

Here's the part people are curious about: **I build with AI, but I don't let AI build blindly.** I use AI the way a senior developer uses a sharp junior — to plan approaches, scaffold repetitive code, and catch mistakes quickly. But every line that ships is reviewed, tested, and understood. AI makes me faster; it doesn't make the decisions. The result is that a simple, well-scoped site can go from confirmed to launched in as little as a day, without the quality trade-off that usually comes with that kind of speed.

![Lines of code on a laptop screen](/blog/execution.webp)

During Execution you're not left guessing. You review real, working pages — not static mockups — so what you approve is exactly what goes live.

## Step 4: Launch & Scale

The last step is getting your site into the world and making sure you can run it without me standing over your shoulder.

Every site I build is deployed to a **global CDN**, which means it's served from data centers close to your visitors — fast everywhere, not just where I happen to be. There's no server to maintain and nothing to patch; static sites are both quicker and harder to hack than the database-driven platforms most small businesses end up on.

Then comes the part I'm proudest of: **the handoff.** I set every client up on an Astro-based site connected to a simple content management system (CMS). It's built to be fast and effortless — and it means you're never stuck waiting on a developer to fix a typo.

Through a clean dashboard, you can:

- **Edit any text** on your site — headlines, paragraphs, prices — yourself.
- **Swap images and add pages** without touching code.
- **Publish instantly** — every change you save goes live automatically in about a minute.

Because the CMS only exposes content and not code, you can't accidentally break the site. And because it's all version-controlled behind the scenes, anything can be rolled back. You get the control of a big platform with none of the fragility. If you'd rather stay hands-off, managed hosting keeps everything fast, secure, and backed up while you run your business.

Discovery, Strategy, Execution, Launch & Scale. Four steps, one goal: a website that does a real job for your business, built fast, and handed over so you're in control of it.
