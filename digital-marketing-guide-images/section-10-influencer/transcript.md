# Section 10 — Influencer Marketing (Transcript)

Source: Udemy "Digital Marketing Guide" — https://www.udemy.com/course/digital-marketing-guide/
Reference only. Not for the blog.

## 1. Influencer Marketing Explained
- Gymshark story (Ben Francis & Louis Morgan, 2012): ~$1,000 budget, sewing machine + screen printer + micro-influencers. Gave free clothes to bodybuilders (e.g. Lex Griffin) for promotion. Went $450/mo → $45,000/mo profit within one year. Still primarily uses nano/micro influencers.
- Influencer marketing = evolution of marketing: cheaper, more effective, highly scalable.
- CPM (per 1,000 impressions): influencer ~$10, organic ~$15, ads ~$30.
- ROI per $1: influencer $5.2, organic $2.75, ads $2.0. (Ads still scalable; influencer good for small budgets.)
- High conversion because it's user-generated content — buyers trust others' opinions over the brand's.
- Huge growth 2016–2024, yet still least-used channel (people don't understand how to do it).
- Influencer = someone who gets people to buy by sharing/recommending on social. Influence ∝ followers. Nano/micro are cheap or paid in free product.
- Influencer marketing = when an influencer helps a business sell.
- Leads into the Influencer Triangle.

## 2. The Influencer Triangle
- Win-win-win system. Three parts: Business, Influencer, Audience.
- Business: has product, needs a target audience. Influencer: has audience, needs something to sell (money). Audience: has money, needs a reason to buy (value/discount) + trusts the influencer, not the business.
- Example: Seth Godin (business, book "This is Marketing") → Tim Ferriss (influencer, podcast) → audience gets 2h of value → buys book → business pays influencer.
- Business reaches audience via any medium (podcast, blog, webinar, course). Value to audience = education, discount, charity, add-on. Business gets money via direct sale, affiliate, awareness, UGC. Pay influencer via flat fee, affiliate commission, free product, performance.
- Choosing the RIGHT influencer is key — product must fit the audience. Niche micro-influencers often outperform mega-stars.

## 3. Find Influencers
- Step 1: understand your niche/audience (market research, GA data, competitor/product research).
- Step 2: find candidates with the same audience. Platforms: Upfluence, Influencer.com, InfluenceCity (paid, optional).
- Instagram = biggest influencer platform. Match niche: YouTube/TikTok for video/younger, Facebook, etc.
- Example: plant-based skincare → search Instagram #plantbasedskincare → vet profiles.
- Check profile: enough followers? right location (esp. if local)? niche fit? branding fit? Watch for overly sales-y "TV commercial" feeds (bad sign — should feel organic).
- ENGAGEMENT RATE > follower count. Engaged followers = actionable audience.
  - Influencer 1: 50k followers × 1.5% = 750 engaged. Influencer 2: 5k × 15% = 750 engaged. Smaller one is cheaper → smarter.
  - Calc: (avg likes + comments on recent non-viral posts) ÷ followers × 100.
  - Example: 600 engagements ÷ 41,000 = 1.46% (below ~5% average for that size → skip).
  - Example nano: 2,227 followers @ 6.71% (above avg) → great fit.

## 4. Paying Influencers
- Why would they choose you? Four motivators (often mixed): Money, Free Products, Authority, Meaning.
- Money by tier: Nano (1k–10k) $10–100; Micro (10k–50k) $100–500; Mid-tier (50k–500k) $500–5k; Macro (500k–1M) $5k–10k; Mega (celeb, 1M+) $10k+ (sometimes $1M+). Varies by niche/product/engagement.
- Per platform: Instagram ~$300/post; TikTok ~$32/post; Facebook ~$450/post; YouTube ~$500/video. Within a platform, content type varies (photo < reel/carousel).
- Affiliate marketing (performance-based): 1) choose platform (e.g. Impact.com), 2) give affiliate link/discount code, 3) track sales.
- Free products: 36% of nano and 29.5% of micro accept free product as payment (Gymshark model). Great for small/no budget.
- Authority: prestige of association (Michelin analogy). Meaning: shared cause/values (e.g. vegan influencer).

## 5. Reach Out
- Use email (paper trail, more professional; nano without business email → DM).
- Personalized intro: who you are + genuine appreciation for a specific post/review.
- Suggest collaboration (short, gauge interest; offer to send product for a reel review).
- Share business link. Focus on THEIR work, keep it about them, personalize every email (no copy-paste). Template provided but personalize.
- Example: "Hi Sophie, I'm Robin, owner of Blue Light Defenders. Loved your Backbone review... would you review our blue-light gamer glasses? We'll send the product for a reel."

## 6. Signing the Influencer
- Create a paper trail (move DMs → email). Nano/micro don't need formal contracts; mid-tier+ may (template provided).
- Summarize the agreement (e.g. 1 reel reviewing + using product, 1 story, 1 image post).
- Add requests (only if paying / strong position; minimal if free product). Keep broad, don't micromanage creativity. Example: mention it's designed for longer gaming sessions with less eye strain.
- Set a deadline (stricter if paying; collaborative if not).
- Request mentions/tags: tag business, link to product page.
- Keep collaborative/respectful; win-win-win.

## 7. Track & Measure
- CRM tool: HubSpot (free) — add influencers, log conversations, track shipments/deadlines. Avoid double-contacting. Influencer platforms (Influencekit/Afluencer) include CRM.
- Trackers: unique discount codes (Shopify/WooCommerce), affiliate links (Impact.com etc.), or UTM codes.
- UTM: set landing URL (product page), name campaign (influencer_product_name, underscores), medium (paid social), source (Instagram), shorten (Bit.ly). Data shows in GA4: Reports → Acquisition → Traffic Acquisition, filter "Session Campaign".
- ROI = (Revenue − Cost) ÷ Cost. Example: ($3,000 − $1,000)/$1,000 = 2 → $2 profit per $1.
- Start goal ROI ≥ 2. Scalability > raw ROI: $1k @ ROI 9 = $9k profit vs $5k @ ROI 2 = $10k profit. Focus on net profit.

## 8. Create a Campaign
- Full loop: 1) Set budget (free product vs money; start nano/micro w/ free product). 2) Find influencers (match type to budget; Instagram default; pick platform by niche). 3) Reach out (genuine, win-win-win). 4) Sign (align expectations/budget; paper trail; contract template if needed). 5) Track & measure (codes/links/UTM; ROI ≥ 2, then scale).

## 9. Build a Team (optional — for medium budget/team)
- Scale like Gymshark. Start solo with free-product + nano/micro; reinvest profit into hires.
- Roles: Outreach Specialist (find/contact), Retention Specialist (relationships, contracts, shipments, CRM/HubSpot), Data Analyst (performance, insights). One hire can do all three (slower).
- Track costs closely; keep profitable; scale ROI.
