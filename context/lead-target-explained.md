# How to pick the lead number for the guarantee

Before a client starts, you promise them a number: "In 90 days, your ads will bring you at least **X** leads." This file shows how to pick X so it is **fair to the client** and **safe for you**.

---

## Part 1: The kid version (lemonade stand)

Imagine you have a lemonade stand.

- Nobody walks past your street, so you pay your friend **50 cents** for every person he brings to your stand. Each person he brings is a **click**.
- Most people just look and leave. Only **1 out of every 10** people actually buys lemonade. A person who buys is a **lead**.
- So to get **1 buyer**, you have to pay for **10 people**. 10 × 50 cents = **$5 per buyer**.
- You have **$30** to spend. $30 ÷ $5 = **6 buyers**.

Now your mom asks: "How many buyers will you get?"

You _think_ 6. But the first day you don't know the best corner to stand on, and some people only look. So you say **"At least 3."** That's **half**. If you get 6, great, you did better than you promised. If you get 4, you still kept your promise.

**That's the whole idea:**

1. How much does one visitor cost?
2. How many visitors does it take to get one buyer?
3. How much money is there to spend?
4. Do the math, then **promise half**.

---

## Part 2: The same thing with Google Ads

| Lemonade stand         | Google Ads                                                   |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Friend brings a person | Someone **clicks** the ad                                    |
| 50 cents per person    | **CPC** (cost per click)                                     |
| 1 in 10 people buy     | **Conversion rate** (% of clicks that call or fill the form) |
| A person who buys      | A **lead** (a call or form)                                  |
| $5 per buyer           | **CPL** (cost per lead)                                      |
| Your $30               | The client's **monthly ad budget**                           |

### The 4 steps

**Step 1: find the cost per click.**

1. Open **Google Keyword Planner** (Google Ads → Tools → Keyword Planner → "Get search volume and forecasts" or "Discover new keywords").
2. Type the service + city, for example "pest control jacksonville".
3. Look at the two columns **"Top of page bid (low range)"** and **"(high range)"**. Take a number in the **middle**.
4. That is your **CPC**.

**Step 2: guess how many clicks turn into a lead.**

- Use **10%** (1 in 10) as a starting guess for a good landing page with a big call button.
- If the page is weak, or the service is something people shop around for, use **5–8%**.
- Emergency services (a burst pipe, no AC in July) often convert higher, because people call right away.

**Step 3: do the math.**

```
Cost per lead     = CPC ÷ conversion rate
Leads per month   = monthly budget ÷ cost per lead
Leads in 90 days  = leads per month × 3
```

**Step 4: promise about half.**

```
Lead number in the guarantee ≈ leads in 90 days ÷ 2
```

**Why half?**

- In the **first month** Google is still learning who to show the ads to, so results are slower.
- Your **CPC and conversion guesses** may be wrong.
- Half still sounds like a real promise to the client, and it gives you room to learn.

## Part 3: Examples

### Example 1: Pest control in Jacksonville (Launch plan)

- Budget: **$3,000/month**
- CPC from Keyword Planner: **$8**
- Conversion rate guess: **10%**

```
Cost per lead   = $8 ÷ 0.10      = $80
Leads per month = $3,000 ÷ $80   ≈ 37
90 days         = 37 × 3         ≈ 111
Promise         = 111 ÷ 2        ≈ 55 leads
```

**Say to the client:** "We guarantee at least **55 qualified leads** in your first 90 days."

---

### Example 2: Handyman in Orlando (Launch plan, smaller budget)

- Budget: **$2,000/month**
- CPC: **$4** (cheaper clicks)
- Conversion rate guess: **8%** (people compare a few handymen)

```
Cost per lead   = $4 ÷ 0.08      = $50
Leads per month = $2,000 ÷ $50   = 40
90 days         = 40 × 3         = 120
Promise         = 120 ÷ 2        = 60 leads
```

Cheap clicks can mean **more** leads than the pest control example, even with a smaller budget.

---

### Example 3: Emergency plumber in Tampa (Growth plan)

- Budget: **$5,000/month**
- CPC: **$25** (plumbing clicks are expensive)
- Conversion rate guess: **12%** (urgent, people call fast)

```
Cost per lead   = $25 ÷ 0.12     ≈ $208
Leads per month = $5,000 ÷ $208  ≈ 24
90 days         = 24 × 3         = 72
Promise         = 72 ÷ 2         = 36 leads
```

A bigger budget **doesn't always mean more leads**. Expensive clicks eat the budget fast. But each plumbing job is worth a lot, so 36 leads can still be very profitable for the owner.

---

### Example 4: Warning: the budget is too small

- Budget: **$1,000/month**
- CPC: **$15**
- Conversion rate guess: **10%**

```
Cost per lead   = $15 ÷ 0.10     = $150
Leads per month = $1,000 ÷ $150  ≈ 6
90 days         = 6 × 3          ≈ 20
Promise         = 20 ÷ 2         = 10 leads
```

**Only 6 leads a month is too few** for Google to learn, and too few for you to tell good keywords from bad ones. One unlucky week could break your promise.

**What to do:** tell the client honestly. "With $1,000 a month in this market, you'd get about 6 leads a month. I recommend at least $2,000 so the ads have room to work." Or don't offer the lead guarantee until the budget is big enough.

## Part 4: Quick cheat sheet

| Budget | CPC | Conv. rate | Cost per lead | Leads / month | 90-day forecast | **Promise**                      |
| ------ | --- | ---------- | ------------- | ------------- | --------------- | -------------------------------- |
| $3,000 | $8  | 10%        | $80           | 37            | 111             | **~55**                          |
| $2,000 | $4  | 8%         | $50           | 40            | 120             | **~60**                          |
| $5,000 | $25 | 12%        | $208          | 24            | 72              | **~36**                          |
| $1,000 | $15 | 10%        | $150          | 6             | 20              | ~10 (too small, warn the client) |

---

## Part 5: Rules to remember

1. **Always use the client's real budget**, the one they agreed to spend with Google. If they lower it, the promise no longer applies. That's in the guarantee's fine print.
2. **Only count qualified leads**: calls of 60+ seconds and real forms. Spam and job seekers don't count.
3. **Write the number in the contract**, with the start date. The 90 days start when the ads go live.
4. **After your first few clients, use real numbers.** Once you've seen actual CPCs and conversion rates, replace the guesses with what really happened. Your promises will get more accurate and you can promise more than half.
5. **If the math gives fewer than ~10 leads a month**, the budget is too small for a fair guarantee. Say so.
